First Jobs Still Matter

November 24, 2026

We want a $20 minimum wage. We also want a 16-year-old to be able to get hired.

Those two goals pull against each other. No law makes an employer hire anyone. When the floor is the same for a 16-year-old with no work history as for a 30-year-old with ten years of experience, the employer picks the 30-year-old. The teenager never gets the shot.

A first job teaches what school doesn't: show up on time, own a shift, take care of a customer who is having a bad day. People who miss that first rung start later and start behind.

Some politicians say a lower rate for teenagers isn't fair, because a 16-year-old may be helping put food on the family's table. That's real. It doesn't change the fact that the employer isn't required to hire them, and often won't. A law that sets their pay at $20 doesn't give them $20. It raises the odds they earn nothing.

Australia has handled this for years with a pre-adult minimum wage. Its adult minimum is A$26.44 an hour. Younger workers earn a set share of that rate, rising with age. In fast food:

Under 1640%

Australia is adjusting too. In 2026 its wage tribunal ruled that 18-to-20-year-olds doing the same work should earn the adult rate. In retail, fast food and pharmacy, their share rises in steps from December 2026 and reaches 100% by July 2029. The starter scale for under-18s stays.

Here is what we support:

  • $20 an hour for every worker 18 and older.
  • A starter rate for workers under 18, set as a share of $20 and rising with age.
  • The full $20 at 18.

A $20 floor is for the adults who need it to support themselves and their families. A starter rate makes sure it doesn't cost a 16-year-old their first job.

Sources: Fair Work Ombudsman, "Minimum wage increase starts today," July 1, 2026; Fast Food Industry Award 2020, clause 15.2; Fair Work Commission, junior rates decision, March 31, 2026, and phase-in schedule, [2026] FWCFB 222, August 26, 2026.
